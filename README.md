# കളിയുഗം — Kaliyugham

Kaliyugham is a self-contained 3D open-world game inspired by Kerala in the early 1970s. Explore muddy lanes, coconut groves, mud-brick and thatch houses, paddy fields, and a village market. A wooden bridge on the field path joins the river's opposite banks. Canoe fishers cast nets while shore fishers use rods and hooks; swim in deep water or wade through shallows. Hens roam house yards, while cattle, deer, elephants, and other wildlife inhabit meadows and forest clearings. The landowner travels with attendants on foot or by horse cart. Their greetings portray a period custom, not an endorsement of feudal hierarchy. The world and characters are generated with Three.js; no external art assets are needed.

## The living village

- Shop fronts face the nearest road. Stand within five units of a physical Malayalam board and press **L** for its English meaning; a translation hint appears in the equipment HUD.
- Chembu leaf stems attach directly to hands while the leaf stays upright. Cart passengers and mounted riders do not carry rain covers. Conversations are limited to two small pairs, last briefly, then release walkers to their routes.
- Eight children play in small groups near their family homes by day, rest indoors at night, and travel to the temple festival. They are non-combat background characters and are not included in equipment targets or alcohol-shop patrons.
- **Temple utsavam:** on the first day and every other day, flowers decorate the temple from 10:00–21:00; visitors gather from 14:00 and the event runs **15:00–20:00**. Three ceremonial elephants wear gold-coloured caparisons, with seated muthukuda bearers and an idol display on the centre elephant. Five chenda performers animate a percussion ensemble. Press **♪** to enable optional synthesized percussion nearby; it stops during pause and is muted by default. These are original procedural representations rather than a recording or a complete recreation of a particular temple's rituals.
- Walkers step onto clear shoulders and wait for approaching horse carts to pass. Neighbour conversations and social stops use off-road standing spots rather than blocking village lanes.
- In rain, only the landlord's household uses woven palm-leaf umbrellas. Ordinary villagers and workers carry broad elephant-ear (chembu) leaves or go uncovered; sheltered shopkeepers do not deploy covers indoors. This is a fictional visual distinction, not a universal historical claim. Farm carts load visible baskets of bananas and vegetables, travel to market, unload produce and restock bananas, then return to the farm; at closing time they return and park.
- Cart yielding ends when the vehicle passes or the sidestep becomes blocked, with a bounded wait to prevent frozen crowds. Greetings are brief rather than indefinite. Off-road conversation partners stand apart, face one another, and alternate speaking gestures and Malayalam bubbles before resuming their routines.
- Eighteen seed-scattered banana plants grow in dry clear spaces, away from shop interiors, roads, doorsteps, paddies and temple grounds.
- Coconut-fibre coir twisting near the field homes and harvest/wage accounting near the estate add everyday rural work. Press E nearby to hear about pay and historical inequalities. These scenes do not endorse caste discrimination or exploitation, and do not imply that all early-1970s Kerala labour was slavery.
- Estate return paths use the manor's actual rotated gate. Walkers repair blocked destination points and retry stalled detours; village carts move by distance, stop at lane ends, and return to park at night. Decorative ridges remain beyond the playable world instead of covering homes.
- All physical shop and temple boards use Malayalam lettering. Keyboard hints remain bilingual-friendly: **E** purchases equipment, **F** cycles owned equipment, and **R** uses it in the character's facing direction.
- Homes are kept outside the market district. Neighbours pause to face one another and exchange gestures before resuming their trips. Walkers take local detours around walls and nearby people; solid buildings, counters, trees, and visible actors resist player penetration.
- A traditional ക്ഷേത്രം (temple) sits on separate grounds. Visitors bring flower offerings in traditional dress during morning/evening prayer hours; the landlord visits during inspections. The scene portrays a fictional historical class-based access custom: household visitors pray inside and ordinary visitors use the outer courtyard. This is a depiction of exclusion, not an endorsement or a claim that wealth alone determined access throughout Kerala. The map marks the temple in gold.
- Five seed-generated cottages are scattered around quieter lanes, with two named residents per home and earthen doorstep approaches. Their residents visit the village by day and return to their own homes at night.
- Dry vegetable beds beside the rice paddies grow okra, brinjal, and trellised gourds. Gardeners tend them by day and commute to nearby field homes after dusk. The minimap distinguishes these beds from flooded rice plots.
- One in-game day takes twelve minutes. The HUD shows the time and changing weather, from clear skies to monsoon rain. Pausing freezes the world clock.
- Estate employees leave for the wage counter from 17:00 with staggered departures. They stand in line, advance directly as the line moves, receive payment once per day, and return to their homes. The clerk stays until everyone is served.
- Villagers walk between their homes, market, fields, and the kalari, stopping for activities and heading home at night.
- Follow the red diamond on the map to the kalari, where practitioners train in Kalaripayattu. Nearby coconut harvesters climb, drop coconuts, and gather them into sacks.
- The landlord walks inspection rounds, stopping at homes, the market, kalari, and fields. Household relatives travel independently by horse cart and a four-bearer manchal.
- The landlord, guards, and household relatives finish outings in the afternoon and return through the estate gate before night. They remain indoors until 07:00, then resume their visits.
- Kalari partners practise controlled sword cuts and round-buckler guards. Farmers work behind paired oxen and a wooden plough; field workers, canoe and shore fishers, and market sellers commute home at dusk and return after dawn. Canoes dock before their fishers leave.
- Deer, elephants, boar, a tiger, and monkeys stay in the wooded far bank, away from paddies. Coconut harvesters drop nuts, gather them into a large sack, carry it to market, sell, and walk back; unfinished loads are kept overnight.
- Your own saddle horse waits nearby. Call it with **Q**, press **Q** again when it arrives to mount, and use **Q** to dismount on safe ground. Horseback travel is faster; **Shift** gallops. Horses avoid deep water but can cross the bridge.
- Most villagers and all workplace actors return home at night, including kalari learners. Walkers carry oil lamps or burning coconut-leaf bundles; lights and flame motion respect pause and reduced-motion preferences.
- A tiger and two wolves emerge from forest dens after 19:00 and roam toward the far-bank lane. They retrace dry forest trails at dawn rather than appearing or disappearing on the road. They do not cross the river, enter paddies or homes, or attack the player.
- Fishers dock and take afternoon catches to the fish stall, sell them, and head home. The next morning they return to the river.

## Buying food

Start with ₹20. Walk up to the **banana stall** (₹1) or **coconut-water stall** (₹2) while its seller is present, then press **E** to buy and immediately eat or drink. The item appears in your hand, and the wallet and food HUD record the purchase and consumption. Repeated key presses cannot charge twice during consumption. Shops close at night, restock after sunrise, and require enough money. Gathering each starlight fragment earns ₹5. Dismount before shopping.

- The old **ചായക്കട — Chaya Kada** near the market has a stove, kettle, benches, and a Malayalam sign. Press **E** near its counter, then choose **chaya (tea, ₹1)** or **pazham pori (banana fritter, ₹2)**. The shopkeeper commutes home at night; stock returns after dawn.
- **കള്ളുഷാപ്പ് — Toddy shop** opens from 10:00 to 20:00, with adult patrons visiting in the afternoon. Press **E** at its counter and select toddy (₹2). Drinking causes temporary reduced walking speed and subtle camera sway; reduced-motion mode omits visual distortion. Repeated drinks can trigger a brief, non-graphic retching/rest episode. Effects fade with simulation time and freeze when paused. Horse riding is disabled while impaired. These are fictional game effects, not a recommendation to drink.
- The **kalari equipment counter** sells a **bow (₹8)**, **sword (₹6)**, and **buckler (₹5)**. Press **E** nearby and choose an item. Each item is owned permanently for the current game session and cannot be charged twice.
- **F** cycles owned equipment and empty hands. **R** uses the selected item: a bow-practice arrow, sword-practice motion, or raised buckler. Animals in range and ahead respond by fleeing, with no wounds, damage, or killing. Three rabbits also roam the far-bank forest. Equipment use is disabled during meals, shopping, swimming, or riding; mouse dragging still controls the camera.

## Visual style

The game uses procedural 3D rendering with locally generated wood grain, earth, thatch, cloth and water detail, smoother characters and hills, higher-resolution filtered shadows, warm portable lights, and a gradient sky. These improve the natural look without external artwork; this is not photorealistic scanned-asset graphics.

The graphics selector switches between **Realistic** (screen-space contact shadows and higher rendering resolution) and **Balanced** (lighter direct rendering). Desktop starts in Realistic; narrow screens start in Balanced. Both modes use individual cutout leaves and branching tree models, irregular background ridges, pleated clothing, generated physical surface/roughness maps, outdoor environment reflections, and rain-darkened muddy roads. All assets are generated locally; no remote models or artwork are required. Character anatomy and animations remain procedural, so this is a realism upgrade rather than a photorealistic character replacement.

## Run locally

- Install dependencies with `npm install`.
- Start the development server with `npm run dev`.
- Open the local URL printed by Vite in a WebGL-capable browser.
- Create a production build with `npm run build`.
- Run world, movement, weather, and water regression tests with `npm test`.

## Deploy with GitHub Pages

The `Deploy to GitHub Pages` workflow builds the site and publishes it whenever changes are pushed to `main`. To enable it, open the repository's **Settings → Pages** and set **Build and deployment → Source** to **GitHub Actions**. The site will be available at `https://solo-codes.github.io/kaliyugham/` after the first successful deployment. You can also start a deployment manually from the repository's **Actions** tab.

## Controls

- **WASD** or **arrow keys** — walk / swim
- **Shift** — jog
- **Q** — call horse / mount nearby / dismount
- **F** — cycle owned equipment / empty hands
- **R** — use selected equipment
- **L** — translate a nearby Malayalam board into English
- **Space** — jump on land; rise while swimming
- **Drag the scene** — rotate the camera
- **E** — buy and consume food at a nearby staffed stall, gather starlight, or talk with a villager
- **Escape** or the pause button — pause / resume

The game is playable on desktop and mobile browsers. A WebGL-capable browser is required.
